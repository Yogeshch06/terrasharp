# TerraSharp — SIH Pitch

## The One-Liner
**Turn 10-meter free satellite pixels into 2.5-meter intelligence — without waiting for expensive
commercial imagery.**

## The Problem (30 seconds)
Sentinel-2 gives India — and the world — free satellite imagery, refreshed every 5 days. That
revisit rate is exactly what disaster response, crop monitoring, and urban planning need. But at
10m resolution, individual buildings, small water bodies, and field boundaries are barely visible.
Commercial imagery fixes the resolution problem but breaks the frequency and cost advantage —
tasking a high-res satellite is expensive and not repeatable at national scale.

**We don't have a resolution problem or a frequency problem. We have both, and existing options
force you to pick one.**

## The Solution
TerraSharp is a 4x super-resolution pipeline purpose-built for Sentinel-2: upload or fetch imagery
directly from Copernicus, get back 2.5m-equivalent resolution — with the same 5-day revisit cadence
you started with, at zero marginal imagery cost.

## Why This Isn't "Just Run ESRGAN On It" (the differentiators)
This is the section that separates a real submission from a weekend Colab notebook:

1. **Multi-spectral, not RGB.** We process all 4 optical bands (including NIR) together, not just
   a pretty picture — because NIR is what makes NDVI-based agricultural monitoring possible at all.
2. **NDVI-aware training loss.** The model is explicitly penalized for degrading vegetation index
   fidelity, not just pixel-level sharpness — this is the difference between "looks sharper" and
   "still usable for crop health analysis."
3. **Per-pixel uncertainty maps.** Every output ships with a confidence map. We do not present
   AI-hallucinated detail as ground truth — regions where the model is extrapolating are visibly
   flagged, which matters enormously for any downstream decision-making use case (you don't want a
   disaster-response team acting on a hallucinated road that isn't there).
4. **Georeferencing integrity.** Enhanced output preserves exact CRS and correctly rescales pixel
   resolution in the geotransform — this isn't a pretty PNG, it's a valid GeoTIFF that drops
   straight into QGIS/ArcGIS pipelines like any other georeferenced raster.
5. **Live Copernicus integration.** No manual download-unzip-upload cycle — fetch by coordinates,
   enhance, done.
6. **Quantitative self-evaluation.** Every enhancement ships with PSNR, SSIM, Spectral Angle Mapper,
   NDVI/NDWI preservation, and edge fidelity scores — we quantify our own output quality rather than
   asking judges to eyeball it.

## Demo Flow (what to actually click through)
1. Landing page — problem statement, 10 seconds
2. `/enhance` — fetch or upload a real Sentinel-2 chip with visible ground detail (river, farmland,
   or urban edge — **never a cloud-heavy chip**, there's nothing to sharpen in a cloud)
3. Before/after slider — drag slowly, let the sharpening actually register visually
4. Toggle uncertainty overlay — explain the "not directly observed" framing
5. Spectral metrics dashboard — point at the PSNR/SAM numbers, explain briefly what they mean
6. Download the GeoTIFF — mention it's a real georeferenced file, not just an image

## Anticipated Judge Questions — Answer These Before They're Asked

**"How is this different from just using a pretrained ESRGAN/Real-ESRGAN?"**
Those are trained on natural photographs (faces, general scenes). They hallucinate texture that
looks photorealistic but is spectrally meaningless — run one on a 4-band satellite tile and you
lose the NIR band entirely, and whatever RGB output you get has no relationship to real reflectance
values. Ours is trained end-to-end on multispectral remote-sensing data with a loss function that
explicitly protects the metrics people in this domain actually care about (NDVI, spectral angle).

**"What's your training data, really?"**
Be direct: Wald's protocol — synthetic LR/HR pairs generated by degrading real Sentinel-2 imagery,
because no free 2.5m ground truth exists for arbitrary global coverage. This is the standard,
peer-reviewed methodology in the remote-sensing super-resolution literature, not an improvised
workaround. Cite it by name; it signals you understand the field rather than guessed at a solution.

**"How do you know the output is accurate and not just 'sharper-looking'?"**
Point to the metrics pipeline (PSNR/SSIM/SAM against baseline) and the uncertainty map. Be honest
that without independent true-HR ground truth, "accuracy" claims are inherently bounded — this is
a known, disclosed limitation of the entire sub-field, not something unique to us.

**"How much data did you train on? Will this generalize?"**
Answer honestly: currently trained on [N] scene(s) covering [describe land types]. This proves the
pipeline and shows real, measurable improvement (cite your PSNR/SAM numbers). Generalization to
arbitrary global terrain requires more diverse training scenes — the architecture and training code
already support this with zero changes, it's a data-scaling exercise, not a research problem.

**"What's your latency / can this run at scale?"**
Current CPU inference is ~19 seconds per tile end-to-end including full metrics and uncertainty
estimation — acceptable for interactive/on-demand use. GPU deployment or model quantization
(INT8 export is already implemented) would bring this well under 5 seconds for production scale.

**"Why should I trust an AI-enhanced satellite image for [agriculture/disaster response/planning]?"**
You shouldn't trust it *blindly* — that's exactly why the uncertainty map exists. Frame TerraSharp
as a triage/prioritization tool (which areas need a closer look, where has land use likely changed)
rather than a replacement for ground truth or high-res commercial tasking in high-stakes final
decisions.

## If They Find a Gap You Didn't Anticipate
Don't improvise a confident-sounding answer to something you genuinely don't know — a judge will
generally respect "that's a real gap, here's how we'd approach it" far more than a bluffed answer
that falls apart under one follow-up question. Name the constraint (time, compute, data access),
state the concrete next step you'd take, and move on. Confidently naming your own limitations
before they're asked is what makes a team look mature rather than caught out.
